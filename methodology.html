The Antizionism Detector™

Methodology & thresholds

Working build 179 · September 1, 2026

What the detector classifies

The detector classifies language in the author’s own voice. It does not classify a person or diagnose a motive. A mapped phrase counts toward the result only when the author states, adopts, endorses, agrees with, accepts, condones, approves, or otherwise presents the claim affirmatively.

Attribution comes first

Quoted, reported, rejected, criticized, debunked, descriptive, academic, or attribution-unclear uses do not count as the author’s antizionist language. The app may still show that mapped wording appeared and identify the quoted or reported source, but the main result is based only on language the author adopts.

The five substantive criteria

Language is within the detector’s antizionism criteria when the author affirmatively: calls for Israel not to exist; describes Zionism as inherently racist or colonial; rejects Israel’s legitimacy regardless of borders or government; supports or justifies groups or violence aimed at Israel’s destruction; or targets Zionists in ways that exclude or endanger Jews. The rule set also maps the associated libels and structures identified in the current coding guide, including Jewish-supremacy claims, inherent-character demonization, blood/body libels, Holocaust inversion, conspiracy/control claims, denial of Jewish peoplehood or ancestry, October 7 denial or celebration, Jewish institutions used as proxies for Israel, and violence justification.

Single-use and slogan language

“From the river to the sea” and “Globalize the intifada” remain single-use antizionist expressions when used affirmatively by the author. “Free Palestine” is also classified when used affirmatively by the author; a standalone use is treated as A-low rather than equated with elimination, conspiracy, exclusion, libel, or violence language.

Occupation, boycott, and genocide language

Affirmative author use of BDS, academic or cultural boycott calls, “End the occupation,” “open-air prison,” and end/lift-blockade or siege formulations is mapped under the current coding guide. An accusation that Israel or Zionism is committing genocide also counts when the author affirmatively adopts, endorses, accepts, approves, affirms, or presents that accusation as correct. Merely naming, quoting, reporting, studying, historically discussing, or rejecting these expressions or accusations does not count.

Control, loyalty, and proxy language

The detector maps control claims that portray a lobby, donor class, powerful few, Zionists, Israel, or Jewish institutions as secretly steering government, media, universities, elections, or policy. Ordinary statements that a named organization lobbies, spends money, advocates, or influences politics are not automatically the same as ownership, puppet, hidden-control, or dictation claims. Dual-loyalty charges and demands that Jews or Jewish institutions disavow Israel or Zionism as a condition of participation are also mapped when affirmatively stated.

Object widening, proxy fixation, and grandiosity

The app records observable language that widens a specific act into a claim about what Israel or Zionism inherently is; imposes Israel/Zionism loyalty tests on Jews in unrelated settings; or gives Israel/Zionism grandiose or uniquely evil responsibility. Broader fixation or double-standard judgments are not inferred from a single phrase without explicit mapped language. These are language features, not psychological diagnoses or person-level findings.

Ordinary policy criticism

Criticism of a government, minister, military action, settlement policy, ceasefire decision, humanitarian conduct, law, or negotiated political arrangement is not automatically antizionist. The detector distinguishes a bounded criticism of changeable conduct from language that widens the object to Jewish statehood, Zionism, Zionists as a class, or Jews and Jewish institutions as proxies.

Version control
