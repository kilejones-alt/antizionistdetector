The Antizionism Detector™

Limits & Use

Last updated: July 31, 2026

Purpose

The Antizionism Detector is a rule-based text-analysis tool. It checks pasted text, PDF/DOCX/TXT documents, JPG/PNG/WebP images, individual public web pages, and bounded public website crawls for mapped antizionist expression and related Jew-hatred tropes. It is designed for education, research, writing, editorial review, and close reading of public language.

What the app does

The app analyzes wording. It looks for terms, slogans, frames, and combinations of language that match the detector’s criteria. It also identifies whether mapped language is the author’s own affirmative claim or is instead quoted, reported, rejected, descriptive, academic, or attribution-unclear.

Web-page limits

Text-based PDFs use their selectable text. Image-only PDF pages and image files use English OCR in the browser. OCR can be slow, may misread handwriting, low-resolution images, unusual layouts, non-English text, or distorted typography, and is limited to 30 scanned PDF pages per file. OCR requires an internet connection to load the recognition engine and language model.

The single-page scanner retrieves one public HTTP or HTTPS page through the configured web-reader service. The website crawler follows same-origin links found in retrieved pages and scans up to 20 pages per crawl. It does not guarantee complete coverage of every page on a domain. Neither scanner bypasses logins, paywalls, CAPTCHAs, or private sessions. Some public sites may block automated retrieval. Private and local-network addresses are blocked by the app.

What the app does not do

The app does not determine legal liability, intent, motive, identity, employment status, school discipline, platform enforcement, or any official finding. It classifies language, not people. It does not replace legal advice, professional judgment, editorial judgment, institutional procedure, or full review of the surrounding facts.

Use with context

A pasted excerpt can omit tone, source, speaker role, surrounding paragraphs, satire, quotation, rejection, or later correction. The main result counts only mapped language the author affirmatively states or adopts. Quoted, reported, rejected, criticized, descriptive, or unclear uses may be shown for attribution, but they do not count as the author’s antizionist language. The app does not classify a person.

Political and moral scope

The detector uses a specific interpretive framework: antizionism is treated as a modern form of Jew-hatred when it uses the Zionist category to libel, stigmatize, exclude, or endanger Jews and those connected to Jewish public life. The rule set looks for mapped antizionist tropes, conspiracies, exclusion practices, and violence-escalation language; it is not a general detector for every sentence about Israel or Palestinians.

Safety and anti-harassment rule

Do not use the app to threaten, harass, dox, intimidate, defame, or target any person or group. Do not use one result by itself as the basis for punishment, exclusion, public accusation, or adverse action. If a result matters, review the original source, full context, exact quotation, speaker role, and applicable rules before drawing conclusions.

Legal notice

The app is provided for informational and educational use. It is not legal advice and does not create an attorney-client relationship. No automated result should be treated as a legal conclusion, expert opinion, or institutional determination.

Accuracy

The detector is rule-based and may produce false positives, false negatives, or context-sensitive results. Language changes, slogans shift, and new examples may require updates. The app’s categories reflect the current rule set, not a final or universal taxonomy.

User responsibility

You are responsible for how you use the app and for complying with applicable law, platform rules, school or workplace policies, and ethical obligations. Use the tool to clarify language, not to substitute for judgment.
