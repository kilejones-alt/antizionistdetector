What is Antizionism?

Last updated: June 28, 2026

A synagogue should not have to answer for a war in the Middle East. A student should not need an alibi for belonging to Hillel. A museum, restaurant, film screening, charity event, or campus coalition should not turn into a proxy battlefield because someone nearby can be called a Zionist.

Yet this is where contemporary antizionism has led.

Antizionism is not mainly a view about Israeli policy. It is not mainly a debate about the philosophy of Zionism. Modern antizionism is a form of Jew-hatred that uses the Zionist category to rename the Jew. The target may appear as a state, a flag, a lobby, a donor, a campus group, a synagogue, a hostage poster, a professor, or a student wearing a Star of David. The method is older than the word.

The old libels have not disappeared. They have changed clothes.

The Zionist is cast as colonizer, child-killer, supremacist, militarist, imperialist, white European, propagandist, manipulator, hidden ruler, and enemy of human progress. In harsher slang, “Zio” compresses the same move into a sneer. “Zionist” starts doing the work once assigned to “Jew.”

This is why modern antizionism must be separated from older Jewish anti-Zionism before the founding of Israel. Pre-state Jewish anti-Zionism belonged to arguments inside Jewish life: theology, exile, emancipation, socialism, nationalism, prudence, danger, survival. Modern antizionism is different. It is an outside campaign that marks Jews and those connected to them for suspicion, exclusion, and punishment.

There are three related forms of Jew-hatred.

Anti-Judaism attacks the Jewish faith.

Antisemitism attacks Jews as race, bloodline, nation, conspiracy, disease, money power, or hidden hand.

Antizionism attacks Jews through the Zionist category: the Jew recast as colonial invader, racial oppressor, genocidal actor, lobbyist, global manipulator, rich white power, and illegitimate presence.

These forms overlap. They borrow from one another. They mutate. But they are not the same. Antizionism is not a footnote to antisemitism and not a policy dispute in disguise. It is a modern form with its own history, slogans, rituals, carriers, targets, and respectable places willing to excuse it.

That history matters.

Soviet state propaganda supplied one inheritance. After Israel broke with Moscow, and especially after 1967, the Soviet campaign turned Zionism into a master slur. Zionism was linked to racism, fascism, imperialism, colonialism, Nazism, and Western domination. This was not careful analysis of Israeli conduct. It was reversal. The movement for Jewish national restoration was recast as the enemy of liberation. Jewish self-determination was recoded as racism. Old suspicion found a new mask.

Arab nationalism and Islamism supplied another. Arab nationalist antizionism rejected Israel as an alien intrusion into the Arab world. Islamist movements added sacred grievance. In that register, the conflict was not only about borders or diplomacy. Jewish independence marked a humiliation to undo, not a claim to bargain with.

The Western left supplied the third. Soviet and Third Worldist language passed into universities, activist networks, unions, NGOs, churches, and protest culture. This is one reason antizionism often otherizes Jews from the left, even while older antisemitism is more often recognized from the right. Israel was placed inside a story of colonizer and colonized, white and nonwhite, oppressor and oppressed. Inside that story, Jews lose their own history. Refugees from Arab and Muslim lands disappear.

Mizrahi Jews disappear. The ancient bond to the land disappears. Hamas and other rejectionist movements are renamed resistance. Israelis are cast as settlers. Zionists are pushed outside the circle of decency.

Together, these strands produced more than an opinion about Israel. They produced an activist culture with its own vocabulary, liturgy, purity tests, boycotts, and habits of exclusion. Its central libels are colonizer, apartheid, and genocide.

Disproportionate Israel fixation

Antizionism can also be recognized by the way Israel is made into a fetishized negative object: not one state among many, but the symbolic center of colonialism, racism, fascism, capitalism, militarism, or world evil. The subject receives ritual attention far beyond its factual scale, and denunciation of Israel becomes a badge of belonging, moral purity, or political credibility.

Hyperfixation alone is not enough. Journalists, scholars, Israelis, Palestinians, Jewish organizations, and regional specialists may properly focus on Israel. The indicator is the combination of concentrated attention with the antizionist lexicon, constitutive libels, elimination or exclusion language, ritualized slogans, collective guilt, or repeated redirection of unrelated issues back to Israel and Zionism.

Double standards and the infinitely rising bar

Double standards appear when Israel is required to satisfy conditions not demanded of comparable states, national movements, or conflicts: defend itself without force, accept permanent vulnerability, dissolve because of governmental wrongdoing, or prove a legitimacy that is presumed for every other country. The detector records explicit comparative language and recurring asymmetry when it is present in the analyzed material.

Israel grandiosity

Antizionist language often exaggerates Israel’s causal reach and explanatory power. Israel or Zionism is made responsible for unrelated wars, Western politics, policing, racism, media, finance, universities, or global disorder. This grandiose attribution inherits older conspiracy structures while presenting Israel as the hidden key to nearly every social problem.

These are language and discourse indicators, not psychological diagnoses. The app reports observable concentration, comparisons, grandiose causal claims, ritual use, and symbol descriptions while keeping them separate from the approved phrase thresholds.

Around them gather related terms and slogans: BDS, anti-normalization, collective punishment, Bantustanization, militarism, chauvinist nationalism, imperialist, empire, western proxy, satellite state, carceral state, open-air prison, ethnic cleansing, ethnostate, Jewish supremacy, ethno-superiority, white European, white settler, Jews-as-rich-white, child killer, Zio, ZioNazi, Ziofascist, Zionist entity, hasbara, pinkwashing, greenwashing, Zionism is racism, no Israel in our lifetime, from the river to the sea, globalize the intifada, resistance by any means necessary, AIPAC conspiracy claims, and claims of a uniquely powerful Zionist or pro-Israel lobby.

The danger is not only that these words are shouted in the street. The deeper danger is that universities, unions, NGOs, churches, media outlets, cultural spaces, and student groups often absorb them as the language of conscience. That is how a campaign against Jewish national life gains shelter inside places that would recognize older anti-Jewish slogans at once.

Antizionism also carries conspiracy. It does not only say Israel is wrong. It says Zionists, AIPAC, or the pro-Israel lobby control politics, media, money, memory, foreign policy, universities, speech, and public life. It turns “Zionist” into the clean public name for the sinister Jew.

BDS and anti-normalization add the practice. Collective punishment, Bantustanization, carceral state, and chauvinist nationalism add a left-coded register of libel. Zio-slang, Holocaust inversion, and “resistance is not terrorism” phrasing add contempt and permission. They do not merely argue. They teach shunning, boycott, expulsion, and refusal. They turn the Zionist category into a gatekeeping device: who may speak, who may join, who may teach, who may perform, who may belong.

This is the trap. Once “Zionist” becomes the name for every hidden evil, almost anything can be justified against the people placed under that name.

The targets are no longer abstractions. They are synagogues, museums, students, diplomats, hostage vigils, Jewish organizations, Israeli restaurants, campus groups, and ordinary Jews trying to move through public life. Israeli Embassy staff were murdered outside a Jewish museum. In the Boulder firebombing case, the accused attacker allegedly targeted people connected to Israeli hostages while invoking Palestine and Zionism.

Students report hiding who they are, avoiding Israel conversations, and navigating campus climates where “Zionist” is hurled as an insult rather than used as a description.

Not every protester is violent. The sharper point is this: public language can teach people whom they are allowed to exclude, harass, and sometimes harm.

That is what this detector was built to catch: the vocabulary and practice by which antizionism now does much of the work older Jew-hatred once did more openly.

Anti-Judaism names the Jew as religious enemy.

Antisemitism names the Jew as racial, national, or conspiratorial enemy.

Antizionism names the Jew as Zionist enemy.

To miss the third is to let an old danger keep its new name.
